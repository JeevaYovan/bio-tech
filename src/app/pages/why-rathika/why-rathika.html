Made from what would otherwise burn.

Across Tamil Nadu, banana stems, sugarcane bagasse, and rice husk are set on fire at the end of every harvest. We pay farmers and mills for those by-products and press them into tableware that composts cleanly back into the soil within weeks of being discarded.

Each harvest leaves a mountain of fiber that no one wants.

We all know paper plates are great, but it means cutting more and more trees, and plastic causes more problems than it solves. India produces roughly four hundred million tonnes of agricultural residue every year, most of it set on fire at the end of the season.

Crop burning is one of the largest sources of particulate-matter pollution in the country. It hurts the people living near the fields, the animals on them, and the climate above them. We thought a better outlet for the fiber was tableware that could replace single-use plastic. So we built one.

Five inputs, all of them by-products.

Each material is something a farm or a mill is already producing and would normally pay to dispose of. We pay for it at a small but steady rate, which gives the supplier a second revenue stream.

Completely Animal Safe

By choice or by chance, animals can easily eat it, no problem. Because every ingredient on the list is also an animal-feed product, a discarded plate or cup is not a hazard for the goat, cow, or stray that finds it. There is no plastic film to choke on, no chemical residue to leach, no painted surface to harm.

That detail mattered to us. A lot of "biodegradable" tableware on the shelf is sealed with a plastic-derived liner that animals cannot digest. Ours is just the fiber. Cattle have eaten it on camera at our trial events without incident.
